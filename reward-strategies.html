Rewards, APR, and Real Yield

How to calculate reward rate, APR, APY, and actual staking yield.

APR vs. APY

APR shows a simple rate without reinvestment, while APY accounts for how often rewards are added to the base and start earning on their own. The difference is especially visible in networks and services where rewards are automatically reinvested. Even then, the final result depends on whether fees are deducted at distribution.

Hidden Costs

Even an attractive yield can shrink if the token has a long unbonding period or the operator charges a high fee. In some networks, you must also account for minimum withdrawal thresholds, bridge or conversion costs, and slippage when selling a liquid staking token on the secondary market.

How to Keep Records

A practical approach is to compare not only the expected rate, but also actual inflows by day and by week. That helps reveal changes in the reward rate, validator uptime problems, or a sharp deviation from the broader market. This kind of log is especially useful if the user holds positions across several networks at once.

Yearnsfi Practical Checklist

  • Compare not only yield, but also withdrawal terms, fees, and the service's operating history.
  • Check separately who holds the keys, how delegation is structured, and whether reporting is transparent.
  • Keep part of your capital liquid and do not treat staking as a risk-free substitute for cash.
  • Review your position after major network news: upgrades, slashing events, fee increases, or changes in the reward rate.

Why this matters: an educational approach to staking depends on reviewing conditions regularly. Even with the same APR, two services can differ dramatically in liquidity, validator reliability, and overall risk profile.
